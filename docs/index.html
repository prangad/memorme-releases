1

Why MemorMe

Most of us have photos in six places: a phone, an older phone in a drawer, a laptop, a box of prints, and a couple of online accounts. MemorMe is the one place that holds all of it, and it works a little differently from the services you may know.

Your photos stay at home

When you set MemorMe up, you choose where the library lives: a USB drive, an external disk, or a folder on your computer. Every photo you bring in is copied there, exactly as it was taken, and never changed. There is no account to create and nothing to upload. If you unplug the drive, your photos go with you.

The clever parts happen on your computer

MemorMe recognizes faces, finds "the beach at sunset" when you type it, reads the words on a birthday card, sorts photos into events and trips, and spots duplicates. All of that runs on your own computer. Your pictures are not sent to anyone to be analyzed, and they are never used to teach anyone's AI.

Nothing is held hostage

The free version covers everything on your own computer, for up to 500 photos, for as long as you like. A product key removes the limit and adds your phone and the family library (see Free, or with a product key). There is no monthly fee, and your library is a plain folder of your original files: if you ever stopped using MemorMe, your photos would still be right there, in ordinary folders, with their dates, places and names saved alongside them.

Made for gathering

Bring in a folder, a drive, a phone by cable, a camera's SD card, a box of scanned prints, or the "download your data" file from Google Photos, iCloud, Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, Shutterfly or Amazon Photos. MemorMe skips what it already has, finds the original behind each copy, and fixes up dates and places along the way.

Getting started

Four steps: install, choose how to begin, choose a drive, and bring in a first folder. About ten minutes, and the rest happens in the background.

1. Install

MemorMe runs on Windows. Run the installer, MemorMe-Setup, and it puts everything in place; nothing else needs installing. If Windows says it protected your PC, see Windows may ask if you're sure.

2. Welcome

The first screen asks how you want to begin:

  • Continue free: up to 500 photos (what free includes). You can add a key any time in Settings.
  • I have a product key: type or paste your key. Activating it needs the internet once.
  • Join your family’s library: someone at home already has MemorMe with the photos' drive, and you want to use theirs (see The family library).

3. Set up your drive

Choose where your photo library will live: a flash drive, an external disk, a mirrored drive, or a folder on this computer. Nothing on the drive is erased or reformatted; MemorMe simply creates a MemorMe folder on it. Already have a MemorMe drive from another computer? Plug it in and choose Already have a MemorMe drive?.

4. Bring in a first folder

Open Import in the sidebar, click Add folders… and pick a folder of photos (or drag folders straight onto the window). Before anything starts, MemorMe tells you what it found, for example "1,240 photos and 86 videos (14.2 GB)". Then it copies them in, and quietly begins recognizing faces, places and events in the background while you look around.

Good to know

Smart search and faces need a one-time download. The first time you use search or People, MemorMe downloads its recognition models (about 600 MB) once. After that, everything works offline. Your photos are never part of that download.

Finding your way around

The sidebar is the whole map of MemorMe: Suggestions, Library, Map, Events, Albums, People, Places and Import, then Hidden photos, Recently removed, Games, and Settings. Press ? at any time for every keyboard shortcut, and Ctrl+Z to undo your last change, anywhere.

3

Bringing in your photos

From everywhere, in any order, as many times as you like. MemorMe remembers what it already has, so nothing is ever added twice.

Folders, files and old drives

On the Import screen, Add folders… and Add files… take anything with photos in it: a backup folder, an old laptop's Pictures, a drawer-full of USB sticks. Tick Include sub-folders and let it run. Videos come in with the photos and live next to them.

Your phone, by cable

Plug your phone into the computer with its charging cable and unlock it (on an iPhone, tap Trust or Allow; on Android, choose File transfer or Photos if it asks). MemorMe looks through the phone and asks something like "Bring in the 214 new photos from your iPhone?" Click Bring them in.

Photos already in your library are skipped, even ones that came in another way, such as from an old phone backup: MemorMe recognizes them by their name and size, so only what's really new comes over.

While it copies, you can see exactly how it's going: how many photos and how much so far, the speed, and about how long is left. A big video gets a bar of its own, so it never looks stuck. Large videos are copied last, on their own, so the rest isn't held up: your photos are already going into the library while the big ones finish. On the Import screen the phone shows under In progress; anywhere else it sits in the sidebar, and a card in the corner appears only when there's a question or it's done.

Keep the phone plugged in and unlocked until it says you can unplug it. Nothing on the phone is changed, and next time only the new photos come over.

Tip

iPhone photos that won't come over. On the iPhone, open Settings → Photos → Transfer to Mac or PC and choose Keep Originals. It usually lets them through, and it's faster.

SD cards and cameras

Put a camera's memory card into the computer's card reader, or plug the camera in with its cable and turn it on. MemorMe notices it and asks first: "SD card · 312 new photos and videos (2.1 GB). Bring them in?" Click Bring them in; nothing comes in without that click. Photos already in your library, and ones you brought in from this card before, are skipped. The photos are copied straight off the card into your library, and nothing on the card is changed or deleted. When it says Done. You can safely remove the card., take it out.

Google Photos, iCloud, Facebook and the rest

Under Bring photos from… there is a tile for each place your photos might be: Phone (USB), SD card / camera, Google Photos, iPhone / iCloud, Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, Shutterfly, Amazon Photos and OneDrive / Dropbox folder. Each opens a short, numbered guide written for people who don't live on computers, with a button that opens the right page in your browser.

Every one of these services lets you download a copy of your photos (Google calls it Takeout). MemorMe does the rest: it notices when the download starts, tells you there's nothing left for you to do, and when the file is complete it brings the photos in straight from the download, recovering the real dates, places and captions the service kept, and tidies the download away afterwards. If a Google Takeout export has albums, MemorMe offers to keep them.

What happens as photos come in

  • Originals are kept exactly as they are. MemorMe copies each file into the library and never alters it. Edits, turns and names are kept separately.
  • Duplicates are skipped. The same photo from two phones and a backup becomes one photo.
  • Copies are grouped under their original. A crop, a filtered version, a screenshot of a photo, a resized copy someone sent back to you: MemorMe finds the original and asks you in Suggestions whether to group them. The best shot of a burst goes on top of its stack.
  • Dates are rescued. From the camera's own record, from Google's notes, from the dates hidden in file names (WhatsApp, screenshots and others). A date is never invented from the day you downloaded a file.
  • Places are named without the internet. Photos with a location get a town and country from a built-in list. Street-level addresses are an option you can turn on (see Privacy).
  • Live Photos stay whole. An iPhone Live Photo or an Android motion photo is one photo again: press and hold LIVE to see it move.
  • New photos say hello. The first time a newly imported photo comes on screen, a soft golden sheen passes over it, once, so you can spot what just came in.
  • Who brought it in is remembered. In a family library, each photo shows who imported it.

Old prints, negatives and scans

Got a shoebox of prints, an envelope of negatives, and a few old SD cards in a drawer? This is how to turn them into a library you can enjoy. MemorMe doesn't drive a scanner itself: scan with the software that came with your scanner (or a scanning app), save to a folder, and bring that folder in. MemorMe takes it from there.

Scan several prints at once

Lay three or four prints on the scanner glass, with a little space between them, and scan them as one picture. When you open that scan, MemorMe says "This looks like 4 photos scanned together". Click Split them: each print is outlined, and you can click the x on an outline that isn't a photo, or drag across one it missed. Each print becomes a photo of its own, cut out and straightened, with the scan's date and place. The whole scan goes to Hidden photos (tick Keep the whole scan in the Library too if you'd rather keep it in view). For any other photo, the scissors button in the viewer, Split into separate photos, does the same by hand.

Scan the back too

If a print has writing on the back, scan the back straight after the front. MemorMe keeps it with its photo instead of in the Library: open the photo's info and you'll find Back of this photo. If it got one wrong, It isn't a back puts it back as a photo of its own.

Say when they were taken

A scan only knows the day it was scanned. After an import with scans, MemorMe asks When were these taken? (it's also a card in Suggestions, and in a scanned photo's info). Tick the photos from around the same time and type what you know: "June 14, 1987", "June 1987", "Summer 1992", "Christmas 1987", "1987" or "the 80s". A rough date is fine: the photos show as "1987" or "the 1980s" and simply stay out of On this day. If MemorMe read a date written on the back of a print, or on the photo itself, it shows it under the photo ("On the back: 1987"); click it to use it. It is never used unless you click.

Negatives

No film scanner? Put the negative on a lightbox (or a tablet showing a plain white screen) and photograph it straight on. In MemorMe, open it, click Edit (or press E), crop to just the frame, then choose This is a negative under Old photos. Color and black-and-white negatives both work.

Faded color

Prints from the 70s and 80s often turn red or yellow. Restore faded color, also under Old photos, brings the colors back. Skip it on sepia and black-and-white prints: their brown tone is meant to be there.

The rest of the care

Suggestions offers to turn the scans that came in sideways or upside down (untick any that are fine and press Turn them). Place on map… pins a whole batch to where it happened. In Edit, Fix it, crop, straighten, brightness, contrast, warmth and color are there too. Your scan is never changed; Back to the original is always there, and everything can be undone from History.

The story

Add a caption (who, what, why it matters), record a Spoken memory in Grandma's own voice, and draw a box around a face MemorMe didn't find to say who it is.

Tip

For a big box, do one envelope or album page at a time. Scan the prints several to a page, then their backs, into one folder named after the occasion ("Summer at the lake 1987"). Bring it in, split the scans, answer When were these taken?, and put them on the map. Ten minutes per envelope, and it's done forever. Old SD cards from the drawer go in the card reader: MemorMe skips anything you already have.

4

Finding anything

Type what you remember. MemorMe understands what is in a photo, who is in it, where and when it was taken, and even the words written in it.

Search

The search box at the top of the Library takes ordinary words: beach at sunset, June 2024, Christmas 2022, Sam's birthday, Grandma and Sam at the zoo, favorite photos of Sam, videos, last week. It also finds the text in your photos: typing lakeview diner finds the photo of the diner's sign. Filter by Photos · Videos or Favorites, sort by date taken or place, and click Save this search to turn it into an album that fills itself (see Albums). Search a year, such as 2024, and 2024 in review is one click away.

People and pets

People gathers faces into groups; give a group a name once and all their photos are named. MemorMe puts the little sparkle on anything it worked out by itself, and you can say Not Sam or Not a face to correct it. A stranger in the background? Under Not Sam, Someone I don't know hides them for good. When a child has grown so much that MemorMe made a "baby" group and a "now" group, it asks Are these the same person?; it never merges anyone on its own.

Add a birthday on a person's page (the year is optional) and MemorMe tells you how old they were in every photo: "Sam, age 4" in the viewer, "7 months" on the baby photos. Growing up plays their photos from the very first to the latest. Pets have their own row under the people: MemorMe finds the dogs, cats and other animals in your photos and suggests which pet is which.

Places and the Map

Places lists where your photos were taken and groups the ones with no location so you can pin them in a few clicks. Save the places you keep coming back to as Saved places ("Home", "Grandma's house", "The lake"). Map shows every located photo as a pin; drag a rectangle to see everything inside it. For photos with no location at all, MemorMe may suggest one when it is very sure, from photos of the same trip taken just before and after; nothing moves until you click Put them there.

Events and trips

Events sorts your photos into the days and outings they belong to, and groups days away from home into Trips. MemorMe suggests names from what the photos show, where they were taken and who is in them, such as "Beach day · Lakeview, July 2024"; choose Use it, Edit or No thanks. A name you typed yourself is never changed. Each event and trip has a slideshow and a Photo book, and you can merge events or group them into a trip. Year in review is on the Events screen too.

Albums and favorites

Point at any photo and click the heart, or press F in the viewer. Albums keeps Favorites at the top, then your own albums: photos for Grandma, prints to order, a photo book. Select photos and choose Add to album (or press A); drag to reorder, point at one for Make cover. Smart albums are saved searches that fill themselves: "last 30 days" keeps moving forward, and photos you import later join on their own. Collages you keep gather in their own Collages album.

Suggestions

The Suggestions screen is where MemorMe asks before it acts: copies to group under their original, event names to confirm, place guesses, children's faces to merge, sideways photos to turn, scanned prints to date, and a few tidy-ups: accidental shots (a pocket, a lens cap), one-second videos from a button pressed by mistake, and the blurry shots in a stack next to a sharp one. Nothing changes until you choose, and everything can be undone.

Reliving

A library is only worth having if you open it. MemorMe brings your photos back to you a little every day.

On this day, and a collage a day

At the top of the Library, On this day shows the best photos from today's date in earlier years, no screenshots, receipts or blurry shots, one photo per moment. It starts with a ready-made collage of the day's best moments, "3 years ago today · Lakeview", in one of fifteen styles: Classic, Snapshots, Film strip, Postcard, Mosaic, Gallery wall, Cinema, Journal, Cover story, Contact sheet, Minimal, Timeline, Triptych, Cascade and Then and now. Then and now puts the same day side by side across the years, always with something in common: the same person, pet or place, and it says which. It only appears when there is something in common.

Shuffle picks other photos, Another style another look, and Play shows the photos full screen. Like one? Keep it: give it a name (the place and year are filled in for you) and press Enter. The collage goes into your library, dated beside its photos and in the Collages album, ready to send or print like any other photo. Nothing is kept unless you ask, and tomorrow brings a new one.

Your year in review

From December to mid-January, Your year in review waits at the top of the Library: a collage for every month, the year in a few numbers ("312 days with photos · 41 places · Sam turned 4") and Play the year, the best photos month by month. Any year is there any time, from Events → Year in review, or by searching a year in the Library.

Make a photo book

A PDF ready to print, from an album (its ⋯ menu, Make a photo book…), an event or a trip (Photo book), or a whole year (Make a photo book in Year in review). It has a cover with the title and one photo, then pages of one to four photos with the date and place. Choose 8 × 8 in square or 8.5 × 11 in; it's made at print quality, and you save it where you like. Most photo-print shops take a PDF as it is.

Birthdays and anniversaries

On someone's birthday, a small card in the corner says "Sam turns 4 today", with a few photos and Growing up. Two days before, it gives you a heads-up, and on the anniversary of a trip or an event you named: "A year ago today: Lake weekend". Click it to go there. Each card comes once; turn them off in Settings → Memories if you prefer.

Slideshows, and your memories while you're away

Any photo, event, trip, album or search can play as a slideshow. Photos drift and zoom gently, a soft fade marks a new day, and a new year gets a line like "Autumn 2023 · Lakeview". Prefer it calmer? Settings → Memories → Motion: Calm, Lively or Still.

Turn on Show my memories when the computer is idle and, when nobody has touched the computer for a while (5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes, your choice), MemorMe fills the screen with your favorites and On this day. Touch a key or the mouse and you're right back where you were. Try it now shows what it looks like.

Games

Games turns your own photos into play. Just for fun: Photo quiz (where was this? who is this? what year?), Face reveal (a face comes clear slowly; name them fast), Memory match (easy enough for the grandkids) and Sliding puzzle. Tidy up as you play: Who's this? shows a face without a name, and every answer names all their photos; Where's this? turns the places you keep returning to into saved places.

Sending photos

Select photos and choose Send: Copy them to paste into an email or a message, Email them from your mail app, or Show in a folder to put them on a USB stick. Choose Email size or Full quality; MemorMe shows how big they are and warns when it's too much for email. Your photos in MemorMe stay as they are.

A little editing

In the viewer, Edit (E) offers Fix it, the Old photos fixes (This is a negative and Restore faded color), crop, straighten, brightness, contrast, warmth and color. The original is never changed, and Back to the original is always there. Every change you make anywhere in MemorMe is in History, with undo.

Your phone

Open your whole library on your phone, wherever you are: at a friend's house, on vacation, or on the bus. The only condition is that your computer at home is on.

Using MemorMe on your phone needs a product key (see Free, or with a product key). Your photos aren't copied anywhere: the phone looks at them on your computer, through a private, locked connection.

Pairing

On the computer, open Settings → Your phone and scan the code with your phone's camera. Check that the same short code shows on both screens, allow it, and the phone is paired. Add MemorMe to your phone's Home Screen when it offers; it then opens like any other app. Up to five phones can be paired with one computer.

What you can do

Look through Library, People, Places, Events and Albums, search, see On this day, and send or save any photo at full quality or copy it into a message. Your memories come along too: ages on the photos, birthday cards, Growing up, and today's collage with Shuffle and Another style. The heart works on the phone too; it is the only change the phone can make. Nothing is edited, deleted or added from the phone, so the library can't be changed by accident.

How it stays private

  • Your photos travel in a private, locked tunnel between your computer and your phone, locked twice over with keys that only those two devices have. Nobody in between can open it, MemorMe included (see How your phone finds your computer).
  • The phone keeps only tiny blurred placeholders; full pictures are held in its memory while you look, and wiped when MemorMe closes or locks.
  • Lock MemorMe asks for your face, fingerprint or passcode when you open it, and after a few minutes in the background.
  • Remove location is on when you send a photo from the phone: the place it was taken is removed from the copy, on the computer, before it leaves. The photo in your library keeps its location.
  • Your computer checks a few times a day that the phone app online is a genuine, signed MemorMe release before your phones use it.

How your phone finds your computer

Think of an old telephone switchboard. Your phone wants to talk to your computer at home, but on the internet neither one knows where the other is. MemorMe uses Cloudflare, a company that runs a big part of the internet, as the operator at the switchboard.

  1. The introduction. When you open MemorMe on your phone, the operator carries a sealed note to your computer: “this phone would like to talk to you”. There are no photos or names in it.
  2. Plugging in the wire. The operator plugs one end of a wire into your phone and the other end into your computer. Most of the time that's all it does, and the two talk straight to each other.
  3. When there's no straight line. Some home routers and phone networks don't allow a straight connection. Then the wire runs through Cloudflare's switchboard instead, so MemorMe still works.
  4. Either way, the wire is a locked tunnel. Everything is locked before it leaves your computer, with keys only your phone and your computer have. The operator can see that a call is happening and how big it is, never what's in it: no photos, names, places or searches.

Cloudflare doesn't keep your photos, and MemorMe doesn't sell or share anything about you with them. They run MemorMe's small server and the switchboard; your photos stay on your computer.

Good to know
  • Your photos live on your computer, so it has to be on, awake and running MemorMe. Closing the window is fine: with a phone paired, MemorMe keeps running quietly near the clock (Keep MemorMe ready for your phone). Keep this computer awake while plugged in stops it sleeping; the screen still turns off.
  • Away from Wi-Fi it uses mobile data; Data Saving Mode in MemorMe's Settings on your phone keeps that small.
  • The route through Cloudflare's switchboard has a monthly allowance for large files. If it runs out, small pictures keep working, and full-size photos need your phone on the same Wi-Fi as your computer.

The family library

One library for the whole household: up to five computers at home share the photos on one drive, over your home network only.

On the computer with the drive, open Settings → Family library and click Add a computer. It shows a 6-digit code. On the other computer, choose Join your family’s library (on the Welcome screen, or in Settings) and type the code. Keep both computers on the same home network (Wi-Fi or cable) with MemorMe open; if Windows asks whether MemorMe may use your network, choose Allow.

From then on everyone can look, name, tidy up and bring photos in from their own computer. Photos the library already has are skipped, so sending is quick. Nothing goes through the internet. Each computer asks Who uses this computer?, so People shows who's who and each photo remembers who brought it in. Every computer in a family library needs a product key: its own, or one that covers more than one computer (see Free, or with a product key).

A family computer can also keep a whole copy of the family library on its own drive (see Keeping it safe).

Keeping it safe

Owning your photos means looking after them. MemorMe is built so that mistakes are reversible, and so a second copy is easy.

What protects you every day

  • Originals are never changed. Turning, cropping, fixing and naming are kept beside the photo, never written into it.
  • Recently removed. Photos you remove wait there for 30 days, so nothing is gone by accident. Restore brings them back; after 30 days they're deleted for good and the space is freed.
  • Hidden photos. Out of every screen, but still on your computer, one click from coming back.
  • History and undo. Every change you make is in History with an Undo, and Ctrl+Z undoes the newest one. Things MemorMe worked out by itself carry a sparkle, and a click puts them right.
  • Your library is a plain folder. Inside it are your original files, with their dates, places and names saved beside them in a form other programs can read. Copy that folder to another drive and you have a complete copy; MemorMe opens it with Already have a MemorMe drive?.

Three copies

Settings → Keep a copy starts with three steps, each with a green check when it's done:

  1. Your library, open and looked after.
  2. A copy at home, on another drive or a family computer, so one worn-out drive can't take your photos.
  3. A copy away from home, so a fire or flood at home can't take everything.

A copy on another drive

Click Choose a drive… and pick a USB drive or a second disk (not the library's own). MemorMe makes a complete copy of the library there: every photo and video, and the library itself, with names, albums, edits and History. Then it follows every change within seconds. Once a week it reads part of the copy back to be sure it's sound. It tells you plainly when the drive has been unplugged for a while ("Plug in the drive so it can catch up") or is nearly full. Copy now and Check the copy are there if you want them.

If the library's drive ever fails, plug in the copy's drive and choose Open the copy as your library…. Everything is in it, and it becomes your library; then choose a new drive for its copy.

A family computer keeping a copy

In a family library, another family computer can keep the whole copy instead. On that computer, open Settings → Keep a copy, click Keep a copy of the family library here… and pick a drive. Everything is copied over your home network only, and new photos follow within seconds. The computer with the drive shows "Also kept on Study laptop · copied 2 minutes ago", and says so if that computer has been away for a while or its drive is nearly full. If the computer with the drive is ever lost, choose Use this copy as the library on the other one: the family library lives there from then on, and the other computers find it by themselves. One computer keeps a copy for now.

Good to know

One copy at home protects against a worn-out drive. A copy somewhere else protects against fire and theft. Until MemorMe can do it for you (see Coming soon), a drive kept at a relative's house and refreshed when it visits is the simplest way; it never involves the internet.

Questions people ask

How is it different from Google Photos?

Google Photos keeps your photos on Google's computers and does its clever work there. MemorMe keeps your photos on a drive you own and does the clever work on your own computer. Nobody else sees your photos, nothing is used to train anyone's AI, and there is no monthly fee. You can still open your whole library on your phone from anywhere, as long as your computer at home is on. The difference is that keeping a second copy is your job, and MemorMe helps you do it. The comparison table has the detail.

Is it free?

Yes, for up to 500 photos (a video counts as one), with no time limit. The free version covers everything on your own computer. A product key removes the limit and adds your phone and the family library. There is no subscription to keep paying, and a key can be moved to a new computer. Photos already in your library are never locked away, even if a key is removed later. See Free, or with a product key.

Does it need the internet?

No, for almost everything. Bringing photos in, search, faces, places, events, slideshows and games all work with the cable unplugged. The internet is used for a handful of things, all listed in Privacy in plain words: activating a product key the first time, checking for updates, the one-time model download, the map's background squares, your phone, and the optional web place search, which is off unless you turn it on.

What if my computer dies?

Your library is on the drive you chose, so plug that drive into another computer, install MemorMe and choose Already have a MemorMe drive?: everything is there, including names, albums and history. If that drive is the one that failed, the copy from Settings → Keep a copy takes its place: Open the copy as your library…. That's why we recommend setting up a copy on the first day.

Can MemorMe scan my prints?

It doesn't drive the scanner, but it does the rest. Scan several prints at a time with your scanner's own software, bring the folder in, and MemorMe splits each scan into separate photos, keeps the backs of prints with their fronts, lets you date a whole batch even if all you know is "the 80s", turns sideways scans the right way up, and fixes negatives and faded color. See Old prints, negatives and scans.

Does it change my photos?

Never. Every original is copied into the library and left exactly as it was. Turns, crops, light and color fixes, names and dates are all kept beside the photo and can be undone. Even a split scan stays whole, in Hidden photos.

Can I use it on a Mac, or in another language?

Not yet. MemorMe is for Windows, in English.

Where do I get help or send an idea?

In the sidebar, Report a problem (or Feedback with some product keys) sends your words to the developer. It never includes your photos unless you choose to attach a screenshot.
