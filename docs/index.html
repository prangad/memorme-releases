    Quick start

    Up and running in about ten minutes

    1. Download and install. Run MemorMe-Setup. On Linux, use the AppImage, or the deb or rpm, from the same download page. There’s nothing else to install.
    2. Choose how to begin. Get started free, or click I have a product key and paste yours.
    3. Pick where your library lives. An external drive, a flash drive, or this computer. With a drive plugged in, MemorMe suggests the one with the most room. Nothing on it is erased.
    4. Bring in your first photos. Open Add photos and click Find my photos: MemorMe looks through this computer for photos you don't have yet. Or choose a folder yourself.

    MemorMe needs a computer with Windows 10 or 11, or Linux (the AppImage runs on most distributions; the deb is for Debian, Ubuntu and Mint, the rpm for Fedora and openSUSE), and room on the drive you choose for your photos.

    Important

    Windows may ask if you’re sure

    MemorMe is new, so the first time you run the installer Windows may show a blue “Windows protected your PC” screen. Click More info, then Run anyway. Only do this for an installer downloaded from this page.

    Chapter 1

    Why MemorMe

    Most of us have photos in six places: a phone, an old phone in a drawer, a laptop, a box of prints and a couple of online accounts. MemorMe brings them into one place you own.

    • Your photos stay at home, on a drive you choose, exactly as they were taken. No account, nothing to upload.
    • The clever parts happen on your computer, and nothing is used to train AI.
    • Nothing is held hostage. There’s no subscription, and your library is a plain folder of your original files.

    MemorMe and cloud photo services

    Cloud services are convenient, and this comparison is meant to be fair: they win on two rows.

    MemorMe compared with most cloud photo services
    MemorMeMost cloud services
    Where your photos are keptBetter: On a drive you own, at homeOn the service’s servers
    Who can look at themBetter: You, your other computers at home, the phones you pair, and the people you send photos toYou, and the service under its terms
    Faces, search and tagsWorked out on your computerWorked out on the service’s computers
    Used to train AIBetter: NeverDepends on the service and its terms
    CostBetter: No monthly fee. Free up to 500 photosWorse: Usually a monthly fee once free storage is full
    Works without the internetBetter: Yes, for almost everythingWorse: Mostly not
    Getting your photos outBetter: They’re already in ordinary foldersUsually a “download your data” request
    On your phoneFrom anywhere, while your computer at home is onBetter: From anywhere, any time
    If your house floodsOnly a copy kept away from home saves themBetter: The service’s copies are elsewhere

    In short: your phone reaches your library through your computer at home, so it needs to be on, and keeping a second copy is your job, which MemorMe makes easy.

    1. Keys aren’t on sale yet. To ask about one, write to MemorMe.Development@gmail.com.
    2. Enter it on the Welcome screen with I have a product key, or any time in Settings › Help and key with Enter a product key…. Activating needs the internet once.
    3. New computer? Write to the same address to move your key to it.
    Chapter 3

    Getting started

    Two screens, then MemorMe gets to know your photos in the background.

    Welcome

    The first screen asks how you want to begin.

    Where should your photos be kept?

    Choose where your photo library will live.

    1. Pick a drive: click one, or Choose a folder…. With a drive plugged in, MemorMe marks the one with the most room Suggested. Nothing on it is erased; MemorMe just makes a MemorMe folder there.
    2. Name your library.
    3. Click Set up library.

    Already have a MemorMe drive from another computer? Plug it in and click Open my library… at the bottom of the screen. If a copy of your library is plugged in, MemorMe offers Open the copy too.

    Good to know

    Search and faces need a one-time download. The first time you use them, MemorMe downloads its photo-search and face helpers (about 650 MB). After that they work offline, and your photos are never part of that download.

    Getting started, in the sidebar

    A short Getting started list waits in the sidebar: Bring in your photos, Name 3 people and Keep a copy on another drive (and Add your phone with a product key). Each step ticks itself off, whenever you like. No pop-ups, no tours.

    Easier to see

    Bigger words and photos, or calmer slideshows, for everyone in the family.

    1. Open Settings (bottom of the sidebar). Easier to see is first.
    2. Text and picture size goes from Smaller to Biggest, and makes words, buttons and photos bigger everywhere in MemorMe.
    3. Slideshow movement: Calm, Lively or Still. Still is easiest on the eyes.
    Chapter 4

    Bringing in your photos

    From anywhere, in any order, as often as you like. MemorMe remembers what it already has, so nothing is added twice.

    Add photos in the sidebar asks one question, Where are your photos?, with four answers. You can also drag photos or folders anywhere onto the window.

    On this computer, and old drives

    1. Under On this computer, click Find my photos. MemorMe looks through your own folders and drives (not Windows or program folders) and lists each folder with photos you don’t have yet, such as Old photos · On your Desktop. Nothing is moved or changed.
    2. Click Bring them all in, or Bring in on one folder. An old iPhone backup on this computer gets its own Bring them in there too.
    3. Or click choose a folder yourself (or pick single photos). The folders inside it come along.
    4. When it’s done, it says Your 1,240 photos are here. Click See them. Changed your mind? Undo this import… under Imported recently takes them out again, into Recently deleted for 30 days.

    A phone, by cable

    Under On a phone, camera card or disc, Show me how has these steps too.

    1. Keep MemorMe open. Plug the phone in with its charging cable and unlock it.
    2. On an iPhone, tap Trust or Allow. On Android, choose File transfer or Photos if it asks.
    3. A card appears in the corner, such as Bring in the 214 new photos from your iPhone? Click Bring them in. On an Android phone it also offers the photos people sent you on WhatsApp, Messenger and Telegram, your screenshots and your downloads: tick the ones you want.
    4. Keep it plugged in and unlocked until it says you can unplug it. Nothing on the phone is changed.
    Tip

    iPhone photos that won’t come over? On the iPhone, open Settings › Photos › Transfer to Mac or PC and choose Keep Originals. It usually lets them through, and it’s faster.

    Camera cards, discs and USB sticks

    1. Put the memory card in the card reader (or plug the camera in and turn it on), or put in a CD, DVD or USB stick.
    2. MemorMe asks first, for example SD card · 312 new photos and videos (2.1 GB). Bring them in? Click Bring them in.
    3. When it says You can safely remove the card., take it out. Nothing on it is changed.

    A phone, over Wi-Fi

    With MemorMe on your phone (it comes with a product key), open it on your home Wi-Fi, tap Add and choose the photos. They come straight to this computer, never through the internet. from your phone, over Wi-Fi on Add photos shows how, or how to pair a phone.

    Recurring import: a folder that keeps filling

    Some folders get new photos on their own, like an iCloud Photos, OneDrive or Dropbox folder. MemorMe can keep watching one, so new photos come in by themselves while MemorMe is open.

    1. Under Recurring import, click Choose the folder, then Add a folder…. If MemorMe spots a likely folder, it suggests it: Make it recurring, or Not this one. Find my photos offers Make it recurring on folders that keep filling, too.
    2. Each folder says how it’s doing, such as Watching · last new photos yesterday · 1,204 photos brought in from here. Pause, Resume or Remove it any time; the photos already brought in stay in your library.
    3. Photos kept only online (in iCloud, OneDrive or Dropbox) are left there unless you click Download them too.

    Google Photos, iCloud and other services

    1. Under In an online account, click Pick the service, then the tile for where your photos are: Google Photos, iPhone / iCloud, Facebook, Instagram, Amazon Photos, or More places for Snapchat, Shutterfly, Flickr and others.
    2. Follow its short guide to download a copy of your photos. Google can even make a new copy every two months, with only the photos that are new.
    3. MemorMe notices the download in your Downloads folder and brings the photos in, with their real dates, places and captions.

    Chats, email and other ways

    Chats, email and other ways…, at the bottom of Add photos, has the rest: WhatsApp, Telegram and Messenger chats (each photo keeps who sent it and when), photos from Gmail, text messages from an Android phone, and a Google Maps Timeline or GPS watch to fill in where photos were taken. For prints, albums, slides and tapes that aren’t on a computer yet, Add a reminder so nobody forgets them.

    When it’s been a while since photos came in from a place you use, Import reminders on Add photos says so, with Not now and Don’t remind me.

    Where each photo came from

    Every photo remembers how it got into your library.

    Open a photo and look under More details for Came from: From “Holiday 2019”, iPhone, over USB, Google Photos download, A print scanned with the phone or Sent from another computer at home, with the day it came in. Once people on this computer have added their names, it says who brought it in, too. Click it to see everything that import brought in.

    What happens as photos come in

    • Originals are kept exactly as they are. Photos, videos and the RAW files from cameras all come in.
    • Duplicates are skipped, and copies (a crop, an edit, a screenshot of a photo) are grouped with their original once you say yes in Tidy up.
    • Dates and places are rescued from the camera, the service’s notes and file names. Towns are named without the internet.
    • Live Photos stay whole. Press and hold LIVE to see one move.
    • Then MemorMe gets to know them in the background: faces, places and events appear as it goes.
    Chapter 5

    Old prints and scans

    Scan with the software that came with your scanner, save into a folder, and bring that folder in. Or scan prints with your phone. MemorMe takes it from there.

    Several prints on one scan

    1. Lay three or four prints on the scanner glass, a little apart, and scan them as one picture.
    2. Open the scan in MemorMe. It says This looks like 4 photos scanned together; click Split them.
    3. Check the outlines: click × on an outline that isn’t a photo, or drag across a print it missed.
    4. Click Split into 4 photos. Each print becomes a photo of its own, cut out and straightened, with the scan’s date and place.

    The backs of prints

    Scan a print’s back straight after its front, and MemorMe keeps it with the photo, under Back of this photo in its info.

    Say when they were taken

    A scan only knows the day it was scanned, so MemorMe asks.

    1. After an import with scans, MemorMe asks When were these taken? (It waits in Tidy up too, under Dates for scanned prints.)
    2. Tick the photos from around the same time.
    3. Type what you know: June 14, 1987, Christmas 1987 or the 80s, and click Date 3 photos.

    A rough date shows as “1987” or “the 1980s” and stays out of On this day. Change your mind any time in a photo’s details, or undo it from History.

    Scan prints with your phone

    No scanner? With MemorMe on your phone (it comes with a product key), your phone’s camera does it, on your home Wi-Fi.

    1. On the phone, tap Add, then Scan an old print.
    2. Lay the print on a dark, plain table, in good light. Hold the phone straight above it and fill the screen with the print.
    3. MemorMe finds the edges and straightens it on your computer. Not quite right? Tap Adjust the edges and drag a corner, or a side to move both its corners. A magnifier shows what’s under your finger, and the handles snap onto the edges MemorMe sees.
    4. Then, if you like: Take a picture of the back, say When was it taken? (a guess is fine), and record its story as a memory note.

    The phone’s whole picture is kept, so you can adjust the edges again later, on the phone or on the computer: open the print and choose Adjust the edges….

    Negatives and faded color

    1. No film scanner? Photograph the negative on a lightbox, or on a tablet showing a white screen.
    2. Open it, click Edit (E), and crop to the frame.
    3. Under Old photos, choose This is a negative. Color and black-and-white negatives both work.
    4. For prints gone red or yellow, choose Fix faded color. Skip it on sepia and black-and-white prints.

    Dust, scratches and faded colors: right-click the photo (or use More in the viewer) and choose Repair old photo…. Drag across it to compare before and after, then Keep a restored copy. The copy sits beside the original, which never changes.

    Tip

    For a big box, do one envelope at a time: scan, bring in, split, date, then pin the batch with Place on map….

    Chapter 6

    Finding anything

    Type what you remember. MemorMe knows what’s in a photo, who is in it, where and when it was taken, and the words written in it.

    Search

    1. Type into the search box at the top of the Library: beach at sunset, Christmas 2022, Grandma and Sam at the zoo. Words in photos count too: lakeview diner finds the diner’s sign.
    2. Narrow it down: click the heart in the search box for Favorites, or open View for photos or videos only, the order and the thumbnail size. Click into the box to see your tags.
    3. What you found is a group like any other, with Play, Send and Make. Save this search keeps it as a smart album that fills itself: photos you bring in later that match join it on their own.

    Nothing found? MemorMe says why, and suggests something close.

    People and pets

    MemorMe gathers faces into groups; name a group once and all their photos are named.

    1. Open People and give each group a name. Name a few, and MemorMe suggests the rest of their photos for a quick look.
    2. Put mistakes right with Not Sam or Not a face.
    3. Add a birthday on a person’s page, and every photo shows their age. Add details… there holds the rest, like a wedding day or their firsts.

    If a child is in two groups, MemorMe asks Are these the same person? in Tidy up; it never merges anyone on its own. Faces nobody knows? On People, Who are these people? has Print a sheet for relatives…, with those faces numbered, and Type in the answers… for what they write back.

    The Map and Where was this?

    1. Where was this?, a tab on the Map, shows the photos without a place one group at a time, so you can pin them. A Google Maps Timeline or a GPS watch can fill many in at once: Add photos › Chats, email and other ways.
    2. On the Map, every photo with a place is a pin.
    3. Click Select area (or hold Shift) and drag a rectangle to see everything inside it.
    4. Saved places, the third tab, keeps the places you named: home, school, Grandma’s.

    Events and albums

    • Events sorts photos into the days and outings they belong to, and days away into Trips. Every event, trip, album and person has the same buttons at the top: Play, Send, Make and More.
    • The heart marks a favorite: click it, or press F.
    • Albums keeps Favorites first, then your smart albums (saved searches) and your own. Select photos and choose Add to album (A in the viewer). New makes an album: name it, then pick its photos.
    • Right-click any photo, or select several, for everything else: the date, the place, the event, tags, turning it, and Make and Put away.

    Tidy up

    Where MemorMe asks before it acts. Nothing changes until you choose, and nothing here is urgent.

    Pick one kind to look at: Sideways photos, Dates for scanned prints, Names for events, Where photos were taken, Growing up, Weaker shots in stacks, Very short videos, Accidental shots and Copies to confirm. Where MemorMe guessed, it says how sure it is, in words. A small dot next to Tidy up in the sidebar means something new is waiting.

    Put away

    The photos kept out of the way, one tab each. It always opens on Documents.

    • Documents: screenshots and receipts, kept out of your memories but still in search. MemorMe suggests ones that look like documents; one click puts one back.
    • Hidden: out of everything, still on this computer (and still using its space).
    • Private: only you, with your PIN. The first time, Make private… asks for your name and a PIN of 4 to 12 numbers. It keeps the photos from other people using MemorMe, but it doesn’t lock the files themselves.
    • Recently deleted: gone in 30 days unless you put them back, with their people, places and notes.

    To put a photo away, right-click it and look under Put away: Hide, Make private…, Move to Documents or Delete.

    Chapter 7

    Reliving

    A library is only worth having if you open it. MemorMe brings your photos back to you a little every day.

    On this day

    A slim On this day strip at the top of the Library shows a few photos from today’s date in earlier years, with Play. See all in Memories opens it in full, starting with the day’s collage. Its × hides the strip until tomorrow.

    1. Shuffle picks other photos; Another style tries another look, and the ▾ beside it shows them all.
    2. Play shows the best of each year full screen. Its ▾ has Every photo and On the TV….
    3. Like the collage? Keep it saves it in your library with a name (MemorMe fills one in). A photo you’d rather not see here: right-click it and choose Don’t show in memories. A whole day: its year’s ▾ has Don’t show this day again.

    Memories

    Memories has four parts:

    • Today: birthdays and anniversaries, letters ready to open, and On this day in full.
    • Your years: a tile for each year. Each opens its Year in review: a collage for every month, the year in a few numbers, and Play for the best photos month by month. To send a month’s best photos to someone who doesn’t use MemorMe, click Send the month… on it.
    • Kept and made: one shelf for everything you kept: collages, videos, photo books and gifts, letters, Then and now and the stories you recorded. Any collage can be kept, not only today’s. A book opens in your PDF app; Show in folder shows where it was saved.
    • Make something: a video, a photo book, a holiday card, a calendar with the family’s birthdays, a recipe book, a kids’ art book, a sealed letter, a Story session, Party mode, a game, and Then and now.

    At the top, Play has Start the memories show and Your favorites, and Games is beside it.

    Make something

    From any event, album, person, year or search, Make at the top offers the same things for just those photos.

    • A video with gentle motion, a title and your own song.
    • Things to print: a photo book, a holiday card (two on a page, with your greeting), a calendar (a photo a month, with the family’s birthdays), a recipe book and a kids’ art book. Each is a PDF ready to print.
    • A sealed letter: write it now, with some photos, and it opens on the day you choose.
    • A Story session: your photos one at a time, big, with a record button, for the stories behind them. On any single photo, Record a memory note in its details does the same.
    • Then and now: an old photo beside the same shot taken again. Select the two photos and choose Then and now, or take the new one with MemorMe on your phone.
    • Party mode: give the party a name if you like, then Show the party on the TV: the TV shows a big code until the first photos arrive. Guests scan it with their phone camera and Take a photo or Choose photos; the TV plays them as they arrive, with the code in a corner (press OK on the remote to make it big again). Guests can help name the faces in the party’s photos, picking from the names already in your library, and you check every answer. Guests see only the party’s photos, and it ends by itself after 6 hours.

    A video or a book takes a minute or two. Click Keep using MemorMe and carry on: it says when it’s ready.

    Photo books

    A PDF ready to print, from an album, an event, a trip, a person or a whole year.

    1. At the top of the album, event or trip, click Make, then Photo book…. (Photo book under Make something in Memories starts from your latest year.)
    2. Choose the size, 8 × 8 in square or 8.5 × 11 in; the pages, Simple, Collage pages or Mixed; and the paper, White, Cream or Charcoal. MemorMe suggests a title.
    3. Click Make the book, then Save the PDF…. Most print shops take it as it is. A book holds up to 160 photos; with more, MemorMe picks the best.

    Birthdays, slideshows and games

    • Birthdays. A small card says Sam turns 4 today, with a few photos: at most one card a day, after a quiet moment in the Library. Close puts it away; its ⋯ menu has Don’t show birthday cards. The anniversary of a trip or an event you named gets one too.
    • Reminders. In Settings › Reminders, Tell me about On this day sends a notification at the hour you choose, on days with photos from other years.
    • Slideshows. Any event, album, person or search can play as one: click Play. Show my memories when the computer is idle, in Settings › Reminders, turns an idle screen into one; any key or mouse movement brings you right back. Slideshows can also play on your TV.
    • Games, in Memories: Who’s this?, which names faces as you play, Name your places, Photo quiz, Face reveal, Memory match and Sliding puzzle.
    Chapter 8

    Sending and editing

    Share a few photos, show them on the TV, or tidy one up. The photos in your library stay exactly as they are.

    Sending photos

    1. Select the photos and click Send. Or click Send at the top of an event, album, person or search, or in the photo viewer.
    2. Pick one way out: Email (small copies, ready to send), Print (4×6, 5×7 or a full page; Ctrl+P goes straight there), My phone, Family & friends, a USB stick or A folder… (full-size copies, kept), or Copy, then paste into a message.
    3. Email opens a new email with them attached; add who it’s for and send it. With email in a web browser, MemorMe copies them for you to paste. Want the big ones? Send full size instead.
    • Too big for email? Most email services won’t send more than 25 MB at once. MemorMe says so before you try: send fewer at a time, or save them to a folder or a USB stick.
    • Where they were taken stays private. Email and Copy make small copies that leave out where each photo was taken. Full-size copies are your original files.
    • My phone (with a product key) puts them in the From the computer album on your phone.
    • A month for someone who doesn’t use MemorMe: Send November… on a month in the Library (or Send the month… in a year in review) picks its ten best photos, ready to email or print.
    • Or just drag them out of MemorMe onto the desktop, a USB stick or an email, up to 200 at a time.

    Family & friends

    With a product key, send photos to a relative’s own MemorMe, in another home.

    1. Connect once, by code. Give them your MemorMe code (it’s in Settings › Phones and TVs, under Family & friends), or type theirs. They say yes on their side.
    2. Then Family & friends in any Send sheet sends the photos. They arrive as an album that the other family says yes to first. An album goes as itself; anything else becomes an album named for it.
    3. What they send you waits in Albums for your yes.

    The photos go straight from computer to computer, never through MemorMe’s server.

    On the TV

    Any smart TV with a web browser can show your photos as a slideshow, over your home Wi-Fi. No product key needed.

    1. Pick some photos (or open an event, an album or On this day), click Send, then Set up a TV at the top.
    2. On the TV, open its web browser, go to the address MemorMe shows, and type the 4-digit code.
    3. That’s it. MemorMe remembers the TV, so next time Show puts the new photos straight on it. Rename a TV, or Forget this TV, in Settings › Phones and TVs.

    The TV must be on the same Wi-Fi as this computer, and nothing goes over the internet. The first time, Windows may ask whether MemorMe can use your network: choose Allow for private networks.

    A little editing

    1. Open a photo and click Edit, or press E.
    2. Try Fix it, or crop and straighten, and change the brightness, contrast, warmth and color. To compare, press and hold Hold to see before (or hold B).
    3. Click Done. Changed your mind later? Click Edit again: Back to the original takes every edit away. Every change is also in History (the clock button in the bottom-right corner, Ctrl+H) with an undo. Ctrl+Z undoes your last change.

    Edits are kept beside the photo, so the original is never changed.

    Chapter 9

    Your phone

    Your whole library on your phone, wherever you are. The one condition: your computer at home is on.

    The phone opens the photos on your computer through a private, locked connection; nothing is uploaded anywhere. It comes with every product key.

    Pair your phone

    1. On the computer, open Settings › Phones and TVs and click Pair a phone.
    2. Scan the code with your phone’s camera. On an iPhone, tap Share › Add to Home Screen, open MemorMe from there, and scan the code again.
    3. Check that your phone shows the same four numbers as the computer, then click Allow.
    4. On Android, add MemorMe to your Home Screen when it offers (or choose Install app in your browser’s menu), so it opens like any other app.

    What the phone can do

    • Look at everything. Library, People and Albums (with your events and saved places), search by name, place or year, On this day, today’s collage and birthdays. ▶ Play any event, person or album.
    • Send, save or copy any photo at full quality, and tap the heart to make it a favorite.
    • Add, at home. On your home Wi‑Fi, ＋ Add on the Library brings in Photos from this phone (ones already in your library are skipped) and Scan an old print.
    • Then and now. Open an old photo, tap ⋯ More › Then and now, line the old photo up over the camera, and take the same picture today.

    Editing, deleting and organizing happen on the computer, so nothing is lost by accident. Free up space on this phone, in the same ＋ Add sheet, checks which photos on the phone are safe to delete because they’re in your library and in a second copy. MemorMe never deletes anything on the phone itself.

    Scan old prints with your phone

    1. At home on your Wi‑Fi, tap ＋ Add on the phone’s Library, then Scan an old print.
    2. Lay the print on a dark, plain table in good light, hold the phone straight above it, and fill the screen with the print. MemorMe on your computer finds its edges and straightens it.
    3. Edges not quite right? Tap Not quite right? Adjust the edges and drag a corner or a side onto the edge of the print. A magnifier shows what’s under your finger, and the handles snap to edges MemorMe sees. Done straightens it again.
    4. If you like, take a picture of the back, say roughly when it was taken (a guess is fine), and record its story. Then tap Next print.

    The phone’s own picture is kept with the print, so you can also move the edges later on the computer: open the print and choose More › Adjust the edges…. The pictures go straight to your computer over your home Wi‑Fi and nothing is kept on the phone. More about prints in Old prints and scans.

    How it stays private

    • Photos travel in a locked tunnel that only your phone and your computer can open, not even MemorMe.
    • The phone keeps only tiny blurred placeholders. Lock MemorMe, in MemorMe’s Settings on the phone, asks for your face, fingerprint or passcode.
    • Photos you send or save from the phone leave without the place they were taken (Remove location when sending, on unless you switch it off).
    • Photos go from the phone to your computer only over your home Wi‑Fi, never through the internet.
    How does this work?

    Think of an old telephone switchboard. On the internet your phone and your computer don’t know where the other is, so MemorMe uses Cloudflare, a company that runs a big part of the internet, as the operator.

    1. The introduction. The operator carries a sealed note to your computer: “this phone would like to talk to you”. No photos or names are in it.
    2. Plugging in the wire. Most of the time the two then talk straight to each other.
    3. When there’s no straight line. Some routers and phone networks don’t allow that, so the wire runs through Cloudflare’s relay instead.
    4. Either way, the wire is locked. The operator sees that a call is happening and how big it is, never what’s in it.

    Cloudflare doesn’t keep your photos, and MemorMe doesn’t sell or share anything about you. See it in pictures.

    Good to know
    • The computer must be on, awake and running MemorMe. Turn on Keep MemorMe ready for my phone, even after I close it in Settings › Phones and TVs: closing the window is then fine, MemorMe waits near the clock, starts when you sign in, and keeps the computer awake while it’s plugged in.
    • Data Saving Mode, in MemorMe’s Settings on the phone, keeps mobile data small.
    • The relay allows 20 GB a month for each product key. Past that, full-size photos load only on the same Wi‑Fi as the computer. At home there’s no limit.
    • A product key can use phones from up to three computers. Settings says so, and lets you remove one you don’t use anymore.

    Phone won’t connect at home? See this tip.

    Chapter 10

    Computers at home

    One library for the whole household: up to five computers at home share the photos on one drive, over your home network only.

    1. On the computer with the drive, open Settings › Phones and TVs, turn on the switch under Computers at home, and click Turn on sharing. If Windows asks whether MemorMe may use your network, choose Allow.
    2. Click Add a computer. It shows a 6-digit joining code.
    3. On the other computer, choose Use photos from another computer at home on the Welcome screen. It asks for that computer’s own product key first, then finds the library and asks for the code.
    4. Keep both on the same home network with MemorMe open. Each computer asks Who uses this computer?, so the photos brought in there are marked as theirs.

    Everyone can then look, name, tidy up and bring photos in from their own computer. Photos brought in on another computer go straight to the one with the drive, and nothing goes through the internet. Faces and MemorMe’s other work happen there too, so the other computers don’t need to be fast. If the computer with the drive is off, the others say the library is resting and wait for it. Every computer needs its own product key.

    Family & friends in other homes

    Relatives in another house who also use MemorMe can send each other albums, straight from computer to computer.

    1. Open Settings › Phones and TVs. Under Family & friends is Your MemorMe code, shaped like MMB‑XXXXX‑XXXXX, with Copy and Email it. It isn’t your product key, and it’s safe to share.
    2. Click Send and get albums…, type their code under Connect with a family, and click Connect. They click Yes, connect the next time they open MemorMe.
    3. To send, select some photos, or open an album or an event, and choose Send, then Family & friends, then Send it here beside their name. Photos that aren’t an album yet become one, named by you and kept in your Albums too.
    4. They choose Add to my albums or No thanks. A yes brings the photos straight to their computer, as an album that says who sent it.

    Both computers need to be on with MemorMe open, and each needs a product key. The photos never pass through MemorMe’s server: if the two computers can’t reach each other directly, nothing is sent, and MemorMe says why. The same code lets them keep a copy of your library away from home.

    Chapter 11

    Keeping it safe

    Owning your photos means looking after them. MemorMe makes mistakes reversible and a second copy easy.

    What protects you every day

    • Originals are never changed. Every photo is copied in exactly as it was. Turns, crops, fixes, names and dates are kept beside it, never written into it.
    • Put away › Recently deleted keeps what you delete for 30 days.
    • History lists every change with an undo: the round button in the bottom-right corner, or Ctrl+H. Ctrl+Z undoes the newest.
    • A plain folder. Your library is your original files, with their details saved beside them in a form other programs can read.

    Three copies

    Settings › Keep photos safe shows three steps under Keep a copy, each with a green check when it’s done: your library; a copy at home, on another drive or another computer at home, so one worn-out drive can’t take your photos; and a copy away from home, so a fire or flood can’t take everything.

    A copy on another drive

    1. Click No copy yet under your library’s name at the bottom of the sidebar (or Keep a copy… in its menu). MemorMe lists the drives plugged in now; click Keep a copy here on one with room. For a folder of your choice, use Settings › Keep photos safe and Choose a drive…. When you plug in a drive with room and there’s no copy yet, MemorMe offers it by name.
    2. MemorMe copies everything, then follows every change within seconds and checks the copy every week.
    3. If the library’s drive ever fails, plug in the copy’s drive and choose Details › Open the copy as your library….

    A copy never loses photos in a hurry. Photos you delete leave it only after they leave Recently deleted, and then wait in the copy’s own removed folder for 60 more days. If a lot would go at once, the copy waits and asks first: My library is right — keep the copy in step….

    Another computer at home keeping a copy

    1. On another computer at home that uses the library, open Settings › Keep photos safe. (Or, on the computer with the drive, click that computer’s name beside the drives: it’s asked the next time MemorMe opens there.)
    2. Click Keep a copy of the library here… and pick a drive. It follows every change over your home network.
    3. If the computer with the drive is ever lost, choose Use this copy as the library… on the other one. The other computers at home find it by themselves.

    A copy away from home

    A relative in another house who also uses MemorMe keeps a locked copy of your library, so a fire or flood at home can’t take everything.

    1. Ask them for their MemorMe code, shaped like MMB‑XXXXX‑XXXXX. They find it in Settings › Phones and TVs › Family & friends. It isn’t a product key, and it’s safe to share.
    2. In Settings › Keep photos safe, under A copy away from home, click Ask someone to keep a copy…, type their code, add a note so they know it’s you, and click Ask.
    3. They say yes the next time they open MemorMe, give it room, and choose a folder for it. An ask waits up to 60 days, and they can ask you to keep a copy of theirs in the same step.
    4. Make your recovery key: 12 words, shown only once. Print it or save it, keep it away from this computer, and tick I’ve printed it or saved it somewhere safe. It’s the only way to open your copy.

    Your copy is locked before it leaves your computer, so the person keeping it can’t see your photos, names or anything else. The two computers send it straight to each other whenever both are on. If they can’t reach each other, MemorMe explains why, and you can Ask to use the MemorMe relay; someone at MemorMe looks at each request. Both computers need a product key.

    If your computer is ever lost, install MemorMe on another one, choose Bring back a copy kept away from home… (on the screen where you pick a drive, or in Settings › Keep photos safe), type the 12 words, and choose where to put it. The computer keeping your copy needs to be on with MemorMe open. No product key is needed for that.

    Chapter 13

    If something goes wrong

    Most problems have a simple cause. Start here.

    Tip

    Phone won’t connect at home? Phones now ask separately before an app can reach other devices at home. On Android with Chrome: Settings › Apps › Chrome › Permissions › Nearby devices › Allow. On iPhone with Chrome: Settings › Apps › Chrome › Local Network. Safari doesn’t need it. Then close MemorMe on the phone and open it again.

    Without that permission, the phone always goes through the relay, even at home, and it can’t add photos or scan prints.

    The phone says it can’t reach the computer

    Check the computer is on and MemorMe is running near the clock. Turn on Keep MemorMe ready for my phone, even after I close it in Settings › Phones and TVs, so it keeps running and doesn’t sleep while you’re out. If it still won’t connect, open Connection details at the end of Help on the phone.

    The phone can’t add photos or scan prints

    Adding works only on the same Wi‑Fi as the computer, never through the internet. Join your home Wi‑Fi, and check the tip above.

    Small pictures load away from home, full-size ones don’t

    This month’s relay allowance has run out. Full-size photos still load on the same Wi‑Fi as the computer, and the allowance starts again next month.

    A product key won’t activate

    Activating needs the internet once. If it says the key is already in use on another computer, write to MemorMe.Development@gmail.com to move it.

    Photos won’t come off a phone

    Unlock the phone and keep it unlocked, tap Trust on an iPhone, and see the Keep Originals tip.

    MemorMe says to plug in your drive

    The library’s drive isn’t plugged in. Plug it in and MemorMe opens the library by itself. If the library has moved, click It’s somewhere else… and choose it; if a copy of it is plugged in, Open the copy carries on from there.

    MemorMe stopped unexpectedly

    It starts again by itself and says so. If it can’t, click Try again, and if it keeps happening, Send a report…: you see what’s in it before anything is sent.

    Windows asks if you’re sure

    See Windows may ask if you’re sure.

    Still stuck?

    Click Help › Report a problem in the sidebar and say what happened, or write to MemorMe.Development@gmail.com.

    How updates arrive

    There’s nothing to download by hand. MemorMe checks for a new version when it starts and every six hours, and downloads it in the background.

    1. When it’s ready, a note says A new version of MemorMe is ready. Restart now, or it updates the next time you close MemorMe. What’s new shows what changed.
    2. Click Restart now, or Not now: the update then installs the next time MemorMe closes.

    Uninstalling

    1. Open Windows Settings, then Apps.
    2. Find MemorMe in the list and choose Uninstall.

    Your library on its drive isn’t touched, and MemorMe’s settings are kept. Install it again to carry on; if it asks, click Open my library… and choose it.

    Chapter 14

    Questions people ask

    How is it different from Google Photos?

    Google Photos keeps your photos on Google’s computers; MemorMe keeps them on a drive you own. See the comparison.

    Is it free?

    Yes, for everything on your own computer, up to 500 photos, with no time limit. See Free, or with a product key.

    Does it need the internet?

    Almost everything works offline. The few things that go online are listed in Privacy in plain words.

    What if my computer dies?

    Plug the library’s drive into another computer, install MemorMe and click Open my library…. If the drive itself failed, your copy takes its place; if the whole house is lost, your 12-word recovery key brings back the copy kept away from home.

    Does it change my photos?

    Never. Originals are kept exactly as they were, and every change can be undone.

    Can MemorMe scan my prints?

    Your phone can: Scan an old print photographs it and MemorMe straightens it. MemorMe doesn’t drive a scanner, but it does everything after one.

    Does my phone need the computer at home?

    Yes: your photos live there, so it needs to be on. Adding photos from the phone also needs the same home Wi‑Fi. See Your phone.

    Can family in another house see our photos?

    Only the albums you send them, with Family & friends. A copy they keep for you is locked: they can’t open it.

    Can I show our photos on the TV?

    Yes, on any smart TV with a web browser, over your home Wi‑Fi. Choose Send on some photos, then Your TV, and set it up once. See Sending and editing.

    Can I use it on a Mac, or in another language?

    Not yet. MemorMe is for Windows 10 and 11 (there’s a Linux version too), in English.
